\abstract{Children with juvenile dermatomyositis (JDM) perform Childhood Myositis Assessment Scale (CMAS) exercises during periodic clinical visits, which imposes logistical burdens on families and limits continuous tracking. This feasibility study investigates whether the Reachy Mini robot can serve as an interactive exercise buddy for home activity monitoring under strict edge computing and privacy constraints. Following an iterative Design Science Research approach, we developed a modular prototype with a shared architecture across simulation and physical hardware. The perception pipeline integrates MoveNet Lightning int8 pose estimation ($\sim$34~ms inference), torso-normalised temporal features over sliding windows, and NumPy-evaluated random forest classifiers, delivering real-time multimodal feedback through expressive antenna gestures, speech, and person tracking. Dynamic pose-centred cropping achieved a 72\% person detection rate on the robot's wide-angle camera. GPU acceleration using LiteRT and WebGPU was explored but blocked by unsupported AES instructions on the Cortex-A72 CPU. The prototype successfully recognises four CMAS movements (chair rise, floor pick-up, and timed arm-raise holds) without clinical scoring, alongside general motor exercises and guided coaching sessions with local on-device model training. We conclude that privacy-conscious, CPU-only activity monitoring with expressive robotic interaction is technically feasible, though clinical validation and user studies remain essential prior to home adoption.}
