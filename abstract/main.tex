\abstract{Juvenile dermatomyositis (JDM) is an autoimmune condition affecting children, characterized by muscle weakness. JDM patients regularly perform physiotherapy exercises assessed by healthcare professionals with the help of Childhood Myositis Assessment Scale (CMAS) scale. Such assessments imply frequent trips to the hospital that can be tiresome and inconvenient for patients and their families. This research explores whether Reachy Mini robot can assist in performing such exercises at home.
A prototype was created that uses the robot's camera to track and follow a person, recognize physiotherapy exercises and provide feedback via movements of its head and antennas. The Reachy Mini robot was chosen because it has a camera, the capability to move and computing on board and thus has everything to serve as an exercise companion independently of external processing resources.
Development and testing have been conducted both in the simulation environment and on the real robot. Several approaches to pose estimation and activity recognition have been analyzed to find solutions appropriate for the restrictions of the robot hardware. Results show that the robot can conduct real-time person tracking and recognize exercises on its processor. An optimized approach to camera video processing helped to increase person recognition accuracy from 52% to 72%.
The paper proves the technical possibility of creating a system that can track patients' exercise progress with the help of the Reachy Mini robot. However, the prototype doesn't perform CMAS scoring and needs further work.}
