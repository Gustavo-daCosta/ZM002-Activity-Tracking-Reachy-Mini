\section{Results}

\setcounter{subsection}{1}
\subsection{Pose-model performance}

The benchmark compared BlazePose, MoViNet-A0, TSM-MobileNetV2, ViTPose and
MoveNet as candidate components for the perception pipeline. MoveNet Lightning
int8 achieved an average of 23.80 FPS, with a mean pose-inference time of
approximately 34~ms on the Reachy Mini. This was the most suitable result for
the project's CPU-only deployment target. The model also produces the COCO-17
keypoints expected by the temporal feature and recognition code.

The comparison also exposed deployment constraints. BlazePose and ViTPose are
useful comparison models, but the robot cannot use the available BlazePose
MediaPipe binary because it requires AES instructions unavailable on the
Reachy's Cortex-A72; ViTPose's detector-and-pose pipeline is intended for the
laptop or simulation environment. MoViNet-A0 and TSM-MobileNetV2 were
considered for temporal classification rather than selected as replacements
for the keypoint-based pipeline. Consequently, MoveNet int8 was used for
robot inference, while the shared downstream representation remained
model-independent.

\subsection{Wave recognition}

On two recorded sessions with our own robot (416 windows), the rule-based
detector reached F1 0.811 (precision 1.000, recall 0.682). The NTU-trained
forest, which never saw these recordings, reached F1 0.951 and outperformed a
forest trained on the sessions themselves (F1 0.884, historical only). On
unseen NTU subjects, where the negatives include deliberately confusable
gestures (salute, selfie and brushing hair), F1 was 0.556. On HMDB51 movie
clips it was 0.065, a deliberately pessimistic external check.

These are in-domain scores. A source confound inflates wave and
clapping, both taken from the studio-recorded NTU corpus. On our
robot recordings wave drops to 0.581, the figure to quote for a robot
in a room, and clapping has not yet been measured on robot footage.
Squats are almost never false positives (precision 0.998) but miss about one
repetition in seven (one in three waist-up). Push-ups (19 training groups,
per-fold F1 0.06--0.71) are insufficiently measured and are not demonstrated.
Without crop augmentation, waist-up F1 for wave, squat and
clapping fell to 0.000.

\subsection{Engineering results}

\begin{itemize}
    \item One model file serves both simulation and robot. The six-class model
    is 5.3~MB and the wave model 0.25~MB, with no scikit-learn dependency at
    runtime.
    \item On the robot, the full wave pipeline runs at 8.7 FPS: 34~ms pose
    estimation and 3~ms motion processing (features and forest). The camera
    (approximately 10 FPS) is the remaining bottleneck.
    \item Antenna gestures are rendered smoothly at 50~Hz independently of
    the vision rate. Head and body following reached 75 degrees of gaze,
    compared with 35 degrees for the head alone.
    \item Preflight turned the robot's frequent post-boot state (asleep,
    motors off and camera released) into an automatic fix rather than a
    runtime failure.
\end{itemize}
